## Platitude Kill List

Flag every instance. Each one forces the prospect to guess what you mean. Replace with specifics or cut entirely.

| Word/Phrase | Ask Instead |
|-------------|-------------|
| All-in-one | All of *what*? Name the 2-3 things that matter most. |
| Faster | Faster than what? By how much? At what task? |
| Better | Better at what, specifically? Compared to what? |
| Revolutionary | Prove it with a concrete outcome instead. |
| Convenient | Convenient how? What inconvenience does it remove? |
| Flexible | Flexible in what way? Name the constraint it removes. |
| Streamline | What specific steps does it eliminate or shorten? |
| Optimize | What metric improves? By how much? |
| Empower | To do what, exactly? |
| Seamless | What friction point disappears? |
| Cutting-edge | What does it do that others can't? |
| Next-generation | Of what? What changed? |
| Robust | What breaks in competitors that doesn't break here? |
| Leverage | Replace with the actual verb — "use," "connect," "combine." |
| Transform | Into what? Show the before/after. |
| Innovative | Name the innovation instead of claiming it. |
| Powerful | Powerful at what specific task? |
| Scalable | What limit does it remove? Name the ceiling that's gone. |
| Intuitive | Don't claim it — demonstrate it. ("Set up in 5 minutes, no training needed.") |
| World-class | Unverifiable. Replace with a specific proof point. |
| Platform | Often hides what the product actually does. Unpack it. |
| Solution | Same — what does it solve, concretely? |
| End-to-end | Name the actual start and end points. |
| Effortless | What effort does it specifically remove? |

**The test:** Could a competitor paste this word on their site and it would be equally true? If yes, it's a platitude.

## Headline Patterns That Work

Use these as structural templates, not copy-paste. Fill them with customer language.

### Goal-Framed (what they gain)
- **[Specific task] + [specific timeframe]:** "Build a custom ad server in just weeks" (Kevel)
- **[Concrete outcome] + [removed obstacle]:** "Convert more leads. Create custom landing pages — no coding required" (Unbounce)
- **[Two outcomes they're already tracking]:** "Increase app store conversion rates and pay less for every install" (Storemaven)
- **[Task] + [emotional payoff]:** "Create a website you're proud of" (Wix)

### Struggle-Framed (what they escape)
- **[Name the struggle] → [Name the resolution]:** "Kick bad habits. Get healthy ones" (Second Nature)
- **First-person struggle:** "I'm tired of ______" (beats "Are you tired of ______?")
- **[Multiple pains, rapid fire]:** "Sleep more. Stress less. Live better" (Calm)

### The "Now You Can..." Test
Every headline should be able to complete: **"Now you can ______"**
- If it completes "Our product is ______" instead — it's company-centric. Rewrite.
- Derivation: customers say "I was trying to..." or "I needed a tool that could..." — flip these into "Now you can..."

### The 10X Example
- **3% conversion:** "Accelerate your learning. Develop your thinking through online discussion"
- **30% conversion:** "Organize, share and take notes on web pages, PDFs and academic papers easily, so you can learn faster, remember more and get things done."
- **Why:** The winner names specific tasks (organize, share, take notes), specific objects (web pages, PDFs, academic papers), and specific outcomes (learn faster, remember more, get things done). The loser uses abstractions.

## Mining Customer Language

### Where to Find It (fastest to slowest)

1. **Competitor reviews on G2 / Capterra / app stores** — 30 minutes, high signal. Complaints reveal what prospects care about. Praise reveals what language resonates.
2. **Reddit / community forums** — Search "[product category] + frustrating" or "switched from [competitor]." People are unfiltered here.
3. **Your own support tickets / chat logs** — The way customers describe their problems in support is exactly how they think about them.
4. **Testimonials / case studies you already have** — Look past the polished quotes for the raw language underneath.
5. **Sales call recordings / notes** — How prospects describe their situation in discovery calls.
6. **Customer interviews (JTBD-style)** — Highest quality but slowest. Use the framework below.

### What to Extract
- **Verbs** they use for what they're trying to do ("track," "find," "stop," "figure out")
- **Nouns** for the problem ("the spreadsheet," "the back-and-forth," "the approval chain")
- **Emotional language** ("frustrated," "embarrassed," "finally," "relief")
- **Trigger phrases** — what made them act ("my boss asked," "we lost a deal," "I spent 3 hours on")
- **Before/after contrast** — how they describe life before vs. after

## Customer Interview Questions

Use when the user wants to gather better language but hasn't done interviews yet. These are the questions to recommend.

### Opening (chronological journey)
- "Walk me through buying [product] from the very beginning."
- "When did you first realize you had a problem with [area]?"
- "What were you trying to do when you first thought about looking for something like this?"

### Deepening
- "What will this allow you to achieve?"
- "Why is that important to you?"
- "What were you using before? How was that going?"
- "What other options did you consider?"

### Trigger (the moment of action — push hard here)
- "You said you'd been dealing with this for [time]. What made you act on that particular day?"
- "What was the last straw?"

### Clarification (use whenever they say something vague)
- "What does 'better' mean to you here?"
- "When you say 'easier,' what specifically was hard before?"
- "Can you give me an example?"

## Comprehension Testing Protocol

Recommend this to the user as a way to validate copy before launch or A/B testing.

### The 5-Second Test
1. Show the headline for exactly 5 seconds (don't warn them)
2. Remove it
3. Ask: "What was that? What would it do for you?"
4. **If they repeat the headline verbatim** — they memorized it, they didn't understand it
5. **If they explain it in their own words** — comprehension works
6. Iterate 4+ times in an afternoon

### Keyword Validation
- Google "People also searched for" to find the words prospects actually use
- Cross-reference with SEMRush / Ubersuggest for search volume
- Example: "investing app" has 2X the volume of "stock trading app" — use the popular synonym

### Quick A/B Testing (for low-traffic sites)
- Run the same ad with different headlines on Facebook/Instagram
- Compare click-through and conversion rates
- Don't A/B test until comprehension testing passes — otherwise you're optimizing between options that are all unclear
